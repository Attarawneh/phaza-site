Code-switching

A Gulf service call moves between Arabic and English inside one sentence; a Casablanca one moves between Arabic and French; an Amman one does both in an afternoon. That behaviour is captured and labelled rather than filtered away as noise — because the model will meet it on day one, and a dataset that has never seen it will fail in the first week.

Specification

One Arabic label hides several different problems

Modern Standard Arabic carries government, media and institutional writing. Production systems also meet regional speech, informal text, local terminology and two languages in one sentence. A dataset is only as useful as the Arabic it names.

Variety

MSA, country variety, regional dialect and the local variation inside it.

Register

Formal, professional, conversational, informal or user-generated.

Domain

Government, finance, health, energy, logistics, legal, retail or industrial.

Channel

Document, chat, voice, search, form, image or multimodal interaction.

Language interaction

Arabic only, Arabic and English, Arabic and French, or the mix your users actually use.

Linguistic services

The labelling is the product

Raw Arabic is cheap and nearly useless. What makes a corpus trainable is what native linguists put on top of it — and that work is done in-region, by people who speak the variety being labelled.

Transcription & diacritisation

Verbatim or normalised transcription, dialect-faithful spelling, and vowelling where the task needs it.

Entities, intents & terminology

Names, places, institutions, amounts and dates as they are actually written locally, plus intent and slot labels.

Dialect & register identification

Country and variety tagged at segment level, so a model can be trained — and tested — on the Arabic you mean.

Safety & sensitivity review

Harmful, sensitive and culturally inappropriate content judged by regional reviewers, not by a translated policy.

The pipeline

From raw Arabic to model evidence

  1. 01

    Define

    Country, dialect, domain, task, population, channel and acceptance criteria — written down before anything is collected.

  2. 02

    Source or collect

    Licensable assets, trusted institutional sources, or a collection programme run by teams in the region.

  3. 03

    Prepare & annotate

    Transcription, labels, cleaning, alignment, de-duplication and task-specific enrichment.

  4. 04

    Validate

    Automated checks paired with native and specialist human review before anything enters training.

  5. 05

    Evaluate & iterate

    Model failures become the brief for the next collection round. The corpus improves because the model was measured.

This is the pipeline behind Salam — trained from scratch on rights-cleared Arabic and English, with Arabic treated as a first language rather than a translation target.

Working together

How a data programme runs

  1. A

    The brief

    You describe the system, the markets it serves and where it currently fails. We say plainly what exists, what has to be collected, and what we would not bother collecting.

  2. B

    Sample before commitment

    A small, real sample in the agreed format — with its metadata and rights documentation — so the decision is made on evidence rather than a specification document.

  3. C

    Collection & annotation

    Fieldwork and labelling run against acceptance criteria, with progress visible and quality measured as the work proceeds, not at the end.

  4. D

    Delivery

    The dataset, its datasheet, its metadata schema and its licence terms, transferred into your environment.

  5. E

    Measure, then extend

    Evaluate the model on it, find where it still breaks, and turn that into the next round's brief. Most programmes continue this way.

Questions

Arabic datasets for AI training

Do you have Arabic datasets ready to licence?

Yes, subject to current inventory and the terms attached to each asset. Text, speech, parallel and multimodal assets that are already rights-cleared for training can be licensed directly; anything missing is collected to specification.

Which Arabic varieties can a dataset cover?

Modern Standard Arabic and country varieties across the Arab world — Jordanian first, then Palestinian, Lebanese, Syrian, Iraqi, Saudi (Najdi and Hijazi kept apart), Emirati, Qatari, Kuwaiti, Bahraini, Omani, Yemeni, Egyptian, Sudanese, Libyan, Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian and Hassaniya. Programmes are specified by country rather than by region.

Can you collect Gulf dialects specifically?

Yes. Gulf work is specified per country and, where it matters, per region inside that country, because a single “Gulf Arabic” bucket hides the differences a deployed system actually meets.

Do datasets include code-switching?

They can, and usually should. Arabic–English and Arabic–French switching is captured and labelled rather than filtered out, because production systems meet it from the first day.

What data is useful for training or fine-tuning an Arabic LLM?

Curated monolingual text, instruction sets, domain material and human preference judgements, with controlled cleaning, de-duplication and metadata — delivered as JSONL, Parquet or whatever format your trainer already reads.

What is needed for Arabic speech recognition and voice AI?

Natural or prompted speech with transcription, speaker attributes, dialect labels and the acoustic conditions of the real channel. Evaluation sets for the same varieties matter as much as the training audio.

How is quality controlled?

Provenance and permitted use are recorded per source; metadata travels with the data; and native, domain-aware reviewers judge regional usage, ambiguity and edge cases that automation cannot settle. Acceptance criteria are written before collection starts.

Can we see a sample before committing?

Yes. A real sample in the agreed format, with its metadata and rights documentation, comes before any commitment — the decision should be made on evidence, not on a specification document.

Where does the data live, and who owns it?

Datasets are transferred into your environment under written terms covering training, evaluation, adaptation, derivatives and commercial use. Sovereignty is the point: what is yours stays inside the perimeter that governs it.
